# AI Job Market Analysis and Business Intelligence Dashboard

---

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes the impact of Artificial Intelligence (AI) on the global job market between **2024 and 2030**. Using a **Business Intelligence** approach, the project combines data cleaning, analytical modeling, and interactive **Power BI dashboards** to extract actionable insights related to:

- Job growth and decline  
- Automation risk  
- Education requirements  
- Career stability  
- Salary trends  
- Industry-level transformations  

The objective is to support **career planning**, **organizational workforce strategy**, and **policy decision-making** by translating complex labor market data into clear and interpretable insights.

---

## 🎯 Key Objectives

### Data Preparation and Cleaning
- Prepare and clean the AI job market dataset to ensure consistency, accuracy, and analytical usability.

### Exploratory and Advanced Analysis
- Analyze job growth trends, automation risk, education vulnerability, career stability, salary distributions, and industry-level dynamics.

### Business Intelligence and Visualization
- Develop interactive Power BI dashboards to present insights in a clear, intuitive, and decision-oriented manner.

### Insight Generation
- Translate analytical results into meaningful insights, business recommendations, and policy-relevant conclusions.

---

## 🚀 Project Features

### Data Analysis
The analysis focuses on:
- Net job growth vs. job decline (2024–2030)
- Automation risk across jobs and industries
- Education level and job vulnerability
- Career stability and salary comparison
- Geographic differences in AI impact
- Industry-level opportunities, risks, and remote work potential

### Interactive Dashboards
Power BI dashboards allow users to explore:
- Job growth and decline by role and region
- Automation risk distribution
- Career stability and median salary by job title
- High-opportunity careers
- Projected job openings by industry
- Remote work readiness across industries

## 🔍 Key Insights (Summary)

- Automation risk is **moderate (~50%)**, indicating task automation rather than full job replacement.
- Higher education levels (Master’s and PhD) offer **greater job resilience**.
- Career stability and salary **do not always move together**.
- Manufacturing and healthcare show strong job growth and **unexpected remote work potential**.
- Industry growth must be evaluated alongside automation risk and work structure.

---

## 💡 Business Recommendations

- Invest in targeted reskilling programs for vulnerable industries and education levels.
- Encourage students to prioritize career stability and long-term opportunity, not salary alone.
- Redesign university curricula to integrate AI literacy across all disciplines.
- Promote AI adoption strategies that augment rather than replace human labor.
- Support healthcare workforce expansion using AI-assisted tools.
- Create reskilling pathways for bachelor-level professionals in declining roles.
- Adjust compensation strategies to retain high-stability, hard-to-automate roles.

---

## ⚠️ Limitations

- The analysis relies on projected data rather than real-time labor market observations.
- Country-level aggregation may mask regional disparities.
- Automation indicators are estimated rather than directly measured.
- Limited demographic variables restrict deeper socio-economic analysis.

---

## 🔮 Future Improvements

- Integrate real job-posting data for real-time labor market signals.
- Apply time-series analysis to capture long-term employment trends.
- Incorporate skill-level automation metrics instead of job-level aggregation.
- Use forecasting models to predict future job demand.
- Expand demographic and geographic variables for policy-oriented insights.
